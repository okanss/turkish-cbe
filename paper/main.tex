\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Evaluating an answer in a single language and formulation provides an incomplete account of
logical reasoning reliability. For Turkish, case marking, possessive constructions, phonologically
conditioned suffixes, and flexible constituent order introduce meaningful evaluation dimensions that
sentence translation alone does not control. We present Turkish-CBE, a reproducible framework for
comparing answers to the same latent ordering problem across Turkish and English and across
answer-preserving Turkish presentations. The framework separates an exact symbolic generator from
linguistic realization, distinguishes endpoint from internal and distance-controlled queries, and
evaluates both correctness and stability on explicitly declared denominators. The frozen design
contains 3,000 latent orders, 23,200 source queries, and 5,637,600 derived prompt identities. We
report construction statistics, independently measured lexical/tokenization features, and an
executed exploratory pilot evaluation of two models on 3,024 matched prompts. All 18 canonical
Turkish relation expressions have zero exact surface counts in the retained corpus measurement,
despite positive lemma counts, illustrating the sensitivity of familiarity proxies to morphological
realization. The pilot demonstrates the protocol's central measurement argument on real models: a
small open model answers with perfect cross-presentation invariance that the paired decomposition
exposes as a constant response per language (chance accuracy, chance correction undefined by
design), while GPT-4o shows graded competence --- 72.0\% English versus 57.8\% Turkish accuracy,
monotone decline with entity count and query distance, and a collapse to 36.9\% under one
answer-preserving Turkish constituent-order alternative, the only effect that survives Holm
correction at pilot power. A supplemental quantized local evaluation of the Qwen2.5 instruct family
(0.5B--14B, 16,618 prompts per model across all nine relation families) reproduces this structure in
open weights and locates its emergence: sub-7B models are constant responders, competence appears in
English before Turkish, and the constituent-order collapse re-appears at 14B at $-38$ points of
query-matched paired accuracy. We formulate the paired statistical protocol behind these measurements,
show how valid-pair coverage bounds the interpretation of robustness, and distinguish the
pooled-marginal CBE chance correction from conventional Cohen agreement. A completed two-reviewer
Turkish linguistic review of 360 sampled stimuli finds unanimous semantic equivalence, excludes one
construction on grammaticality, and leaves discourse naturalness contested; the constituent-order
collapse survives removal of the excluded construction. The frozen-test evaluation remains the
prerequisite for confirmatory answers to the research questions.
\end{abstract}
