\section{Introduction}

A logical conclusion can remain unchanged when its premises are reordered, a relation is expressed through
its converse, or a language uses a different grammatical realization. A model that changes its answer under
such transformations presents a different evaluation problem from a model that consistently reaches the
wrong conclusion. Aggregate accuracy obscures both distinctions: equal scores can conceal failures on
different instances, while high agreement can arise from repeating a biased answer. Behavioral tests and
consistency evaluations therefore complement conventional task-level scores \citep{ribeiro2020checklist,
elazar2021pararel}.

The problem is particularly consequential for multilingual evaluation. Turkish expresses grammatical
relations through productive suffixation and morphophonological alternations, and permits constituent-order
choices whose discourse effects are not captured by a literal translation. Computational descriptions of
Turkish morphology formalize the interaction between morphotactics and phonological realization
\citep{oflazer1994turkish}. Recent Turkish evaluations investigate morphological compositionality,
linguistic minimal pairs, and surface-form confounders \citep{ismayilzada2025morphology,
basar2025turblimp, basar2026morphologyaware}. These studies motivate controlled linguistic testing, but
they do not establish how equivalent Turkish formulations affect transitive ordering inference. That
question requires its own matched design and genuine model outcomes.

Competence-Based Evaluation (CBE) treats correctness across presentations of a parameterized problem
family as a behavioral requirement \citep{jiang2026cbe}. We adopt this framing and extend it to
Turkish--English matching, morphosyntactic interventions, lexical measurements, and query-distance
controls. The object of comparison is the same latent instance and question, rather than unrelated
language-specific test items. A failure on one equivalent formulation falsifies the corresponding
finite-instance requirement; success on a finite sample does not certify an unbounded reasoning competence
or reveal its internal implementation.

Turkish-CBE addresses five research questions. RQ1 concerns the paired Turkish--English accuracy
difference. RQ2 concerns sensitivity to answer-preserving Turkish presentations. RQ3 separates entity
count from query distance and query type. RQ4 examines associations among relation vocabulary, independent
corpus frequency, tokenization, and predicted-answer bias. RQ5 asks whether reasoning prompting or
optional adaptation improves correct consistency as well as accuracy. We specify estimands and controls
for these questions without presupposing that Turkish is harder, that morphology causes an error, or that
an intervention necessarily helps.

The present article has four contributions. First, it describes a deterministic, language-independent
ordering generator with exact answers and split-level separation of latent instances. Second, it specifies
Turkish rendering and bilingual matching with explicit limits on what automatic semantic checks can
establish. Third, it provides a denominator-aware reliability analysis and reproducible visual audit of the
constructed data and measured lexical features. Fourth, it reports an executed exploratory pilot evaluation
of two models (Section~\ref{sec:pilot}) that exercises the complete gated protocol and demonstrates, on
real model behavior, why the paired decomposition is necessary: one model achieves perfect raw invariance
by answering constantly, while another loses robust correctness under a single answer-preserving Turkish
construction. A supplemental quantized local evaluation of four open-weight scales across all nine
relation families (Section~\ref{sec:p1-local}) reproduces this structure and locates its emergence with
scale. A completed two-reviewer linguistic review of the sampled stimuli
(Section~\ref{sec:review}) backs their grammaticality and meaning preservation while leaving
naturalness contested; because only the pilot split was used and no execution-gate receipt was issued,
the manuscript makes no confirmatory claim of a language gap, prompting gain, or training benefit.
